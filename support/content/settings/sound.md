## Change the volume

Choose the speaker icon beside the clock and move the slider. The volume keys on your keyboard work too.

The same panel lists apps that are playing sound, so you can turn one app down without changing the rest.

## Choose a speaker or headphones

When more than one output is connected, for example laptop speakers, a monitor with speakers and Bluetooth headphones, you choose which one plays.

1. Open the **Start menu** and type `sound`.
2. Choose **Sound**.
3. Under the output devices, pick the one you want.

You can also switch outputs from the speaker icon beside the clock.

> Note: A monitor connected by HDMI or DisplayPort counts as a speaker, even if it has none. If sound disappears after plugging in a monitor, check which output is selected.

## Choose a microphone

On the same Sound page, the input devices are listed. Pick your microphone and speak. A level bar moves when it hears you. Raise the input volume if the bar barely moves.

Video call apps and websites also have their own microphone setting. If the level bar moves in Settings and the other person still cannot hear you, check the setting inside the app. A web browser asks for permission the first time a site wants the microphone.

## Bluetooth headphones

Pair them first. See [Bluetooth devices](/settings/bluetooth). Then choose them as the output.

## If there is no sound

See [No sound](/troubleshooting/no-sound).

> Note: OneClick OS is an early test version. Sound works on most PCs, but some laptop speakers and microphones are not supported yet.

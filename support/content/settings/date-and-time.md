You set the time zone during first-time setup. The clock then keeps itself correct from the internet.

## Open the date and time settings

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Find the date and time page. Typing `time` in the Settings search box is the quickest way.

## Set the time automatically

Leave automatic time turned on. The PC checks standard internet time servers and corrects the clock, including for daylight saving. Nothing about you is sent.

If the PC has no internet, the clock keeps running from the PC's own battery-backed clock and is corrected the next time it goes online.

## Change the time zone

1. On the date and time page, find the time zone.
2. Choose the city nearest you in the same zone.
3. Apply. You may be asked for your password.

Change the time zone when you travel. Do not change the time itself to match a new place, because the clock will then be wrong when you come home.

## Set the time by hand

Turn automatic time off, then enter the date and time. Do this only on a PC that never goes online.

## 12 or 24 hour clock

How the time and date are written follows your region settings. See [Keyboard layout and language](/settings/keyboard-and-language). The clock on the taskbar also has its own options. See [The clock and icons in the corner](/desktop/system-tray).

## The clock is wrong after using Windows

If you start Windows and OneClick OS on the same PC, the clock can be out by several hours after switching. See [The clock is wrong after using Windows](/troubleshooting/clock-wrong-after-windows).

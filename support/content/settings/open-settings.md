Settings is the one place for changing how the PC works. It replaces the Windows Settings app and Control Panel.

## Open Settings

1. Open the **Start menu**.
2. Choose **Settings**. It is pinned there from the start.

## Find a setting by searching

You do not have to know which page a setting lives on.

1. Open the **Start menu** and start typing in the search box, for example `display` or `bluetooth`.
2. Matching Settings pages appear in the results with **Settings** written under the name.
3. Choose one and it opens on that page.

The pages you can jump to this way include Display & Monitor, Sound, Wi-Fi & Network, Bluetooth, Keyboard, Mouse & Touchpad, Users, Night Light and Power Management.

The Settings window also has its own search box for looking through every page.

> Note: On a slow PC, search results can take a few seconds to appear.

## Pages OneClick added

Most pages are the standard ones. These five are OneClick's own:

| Page | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Look** | Switch between Windows style and Mac style |
| **Taskbar** | Hiding, position and size of the taskbar |
| **Windows Apps** | Whether double-clicking a Windows program runs it |
| **Cloud Drives** | Connect OneDrive and Google Drive |
| **Update** | Check for, download and undo system updates |

See [Change how the taskbar behaves](/desktop/taskbar-settings), [The Windows Apps page](/settings/windows-apps-page), [The Cloud Drives page](/settings/cloud-drives-page) and [How updates work](/updates/how-updates-work).

## Wording you may not expect

OneClick has renamed the everyday apps and its own pages in plain words. A few of the standard Settings pages still use the wording they came with, so a page name may differ a little from what a help article calls it. If you cannot find a page, search for what you want to change instead of the page name.

If you are looking for something from Windows, see [Where Control Panel and Device Manager went](/windows/control-panel).

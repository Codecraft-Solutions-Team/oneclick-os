> Warning: The installer for PCs is not available yet. This page describes how a PC with both systems is meant to behave. It has not yet been tested on real PCs.

## The start-up list

When OneClick OS finds another system on the PC, it shows a short list each time the PC starts.

1. Turn the PC on.
2. When the list appears, use the **up** and **down** arrow keys to move.
3. Press **Enter** on the system you want.

If you do nothing, the top entry starts by itself after a few seconds.

## What the entries mean

- The **first entry** is OneClick OS as it is now.
- A **second OneClick entry** is the version from before your last update. It is your way back if an update goes wrong. See [Undo an update](/updates/undo-an-update).
- **Windows** has its own entry.

## If OneClick is the only system

The list is hidden, and the PC goes straight to the OneClick start-up screen. To see the list anyway, hold **Shift** or tap **Esc** as the PC starts.

## Switching from one to the other

There is no shortcut from one system into the other. Restart the PC and pick from the list.

- In OneClick OS: open the **Start menu** and choose **Restart**.
- In Windows: open the Start menu, choose **Power**, then **Restart**.

## Your PC's own start-up menu

Every PC also has a start-up menu of its own, opened with a key at power-on. The key depends on the maker. **F12**, **F9**, **F8** and **Esc** are common. It lists the systems and drives the PC can start from. It is a useful second route if the OneClick list does not show Windows.

## If something is missing

- Windows is not in the list: see [Windows is missing from the start-up list](/troubleshooting/windows-missing-from-startup).
- OneClick OS does not start: see [The PC will not start OneClick OS](/troubleshooting/wont-start).

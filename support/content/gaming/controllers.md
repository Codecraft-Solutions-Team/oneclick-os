Support for common game controllers is built into OneClick OS. You do not install drivers or download a companion app.

## Connect by cable

Plug the controller into a USB port. Give it a few seconds, then open your game. This is the most reliable way and the best one to try first.

## Connect by Bluetooth

1. Put the controller in pairing mode. On an Xbox controller, hold the small pair button on the top edge until the light flashes quickly. On a PlayStation controller, hold the **Share** or **Create** button and the **PS** button together until the light flashes.
2. On the PC, open **Settings** and choose **Bluetooth**.
3. Add a new device and pick the controller from the list.

For more detail, see [Bluetooth devices](/settings/bluetooth).

## Which controllers

| Controller | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Xbox One and Xbox Series | Usually works by cable and Bluetooth |
| PlayStation 4 and 5 | Usually works by cable and Bluetooth |
| Nintendo Switch Pro | Usually works. Button names may be swapped until you set it up in Steam |
| Other brands | Most that work on a PC without special software work here |

> Note: OneClick OS is an early test version. Controllers have not been tested model by model. The Xbox Wireless Adapter, the small USB receiver sold for Xbox controllers, may not work. If it does not, use a cable or Bluetooth.

## Set it up in Steam

Steam has its own controller settings, and they are the easiest way to sort out button layouts.

1. Open **Steam**.
2. Open **Settings** and choose **Controller**.
3. Check that your controller is listed. From here you can test the buttons and change the layout.

Steam can also make one kind of controller look like another to a game, which helps with older games that expect an Xbox controller.

## In cloud gaming

GeForce NOW and Xbox Cloud Gaming read the controller through the web browser. Connect the controller before you open the page. If the page does not see it, press a button on the controller, then try a Chrome-based browser. See [Xbox Cloud Gaming](/gaming/xbox-cloud-gaming).

## If a controller is not picked up

- Try a different USB port and a different cable. Some cables only charge.
- Remove the controller in **Settings**, **Bluetooth**, and pair it again.
- Restart the game after connecting. Many games only look for controllers when they start.

If Bluetooth itself is the problem, see [Bluetooth will not connect](/troubleshooting/bluetooth-not-working).

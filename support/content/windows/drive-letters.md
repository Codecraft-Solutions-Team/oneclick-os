OneClick OS does not use drive letters. There is no C drive, no D drive, and no "This PC" full of letters. Everything you need is in **Files**, under plain names.

## Where your files are

Your own files live in your **Home** folder. Open **Files** and it is the first place you see. Inside are the folders you know:

- Desktop
- Documents
- Downloads
- Music
- Pictures
- Videos

This is the same idea as your user folder in Windows, the one at C, Users, your name.

## Where other drives are

USB sticks, external drives, a second disk and a Windows disk all appear in the sidebar of **Files**. Each is shown by its name or its size. Click one to open it.

Before you unplug a USB stick or external drive, remove it safely from the sidebar first. See [USB sticks and external drives](/desktop/external-drives).

## Where the system is

The system keeps its own files apart from yours. You do not need to go there, and most of it cannot be changed by hand. That is deliberate. It is what lets an update be undone cleanly. See [How updates work](/updates/how-updates-work).

There is no Program Files folder to look after. Apps are installed and removed in the **App Store**.

## What a file's address looks like

| | Windows | OneClick OS |
|---|---|---|
| Separator | Backslash | Forward slash |
| A document | `C:\Users\Sam\Documents\letter.docx` | `/home/sam/Documents/letter.docx` |

You rarely need to type an address. If an app asks you where to save, pick a folder from the list as you would in Windows.

Remember that capital letters matter here. `Letter.docx` and `letter.docx` are two different files.

## How much space is left

- **Files** shows the free space of the place you are looking at.
- **Disk Usage**, in the Start menu, shows what is taking up the room.

See [See what is using your disk space](/settings/storage).

summary: Updates are your decision, and here is the reasoning behind that.

On OneClick OS nothing downloads, installs or restarts for an update unless you ask. The **Update** page says it in one line: "Updates are never installed without you choosing to."

## What that means in practice

- The PC does not download system updates in the background.
- It does not restart by itself to finish one.
- Shutting down does not turn into "installing updates, do not turn off your computer".
- There is no deadline after which an update installs anyway.
- You can stay on a version you like and receive only security fixes for it.

## Why it is built this way

**It is your computer.** A restart in the middle of your work, a game or a presentation is a decision someone else made about your time.

**Updates change things.** A new version can move a button or change how something behaves. You should be able to choose when that happens, or choose that it does not.

**Not everyone has fast, unlimited internet.** System updates can be large. The page shows the size in megabytes before anything is downloaded, so you can wait until you are on a suitable connection.

**An update can go wrong.** Because you chose the moment, you know what changed and when. And because the previous version is kept, you can go back. See [Undo an update](/updates/undo-an-update).

## The trade-off

If nothing is forced, staying up to date is up to you. A PC that is never updated misses security fixes.

A sensible habit is to open **Update** and press **Check now** every week or two. If you do not want new features, choose **Security only** so that the only thing you receive is security fixes. See [Full updates or security fixes only](/updates/full-or-security).

## Apps are separate

Apps are updated in the **App Store**, not on the Update page. See [Update your apps](/apps/update-apps).

If a laptop is lost or stolen, the question is whether a stranger can read what is on it. This page explains what protects you and what does not.

## What your password does

Your account password stops someone signing in at the sign-in screen or getting past the lock screen.

It does not scramble the files on the disk. Someone who takes the disk out, or starts the PC from a USB stick, can read files that are not encrypted without knowing your password. This is true of any computer, including a Windows PC without BitLocker.

## What disk encryption adds

Disk encryption scrambles everything on the disk. Without the right passphrase the contents cannot be read, even with the disk in another computer.

OneClick OS does not turn disk encryption on by itself. Whether your disk is encrypted depends on what was chosen when OneClick OS was installed.

## How to tell if your disk is encrypted

If the disk is encrypted, the PC asks for a passphrase every time it starts, before the sign-in screen appears. If your PC goes straight to the sign-in screen or the desktop, the disk is not encrypted.

## If it is not encrypted

Encryption is normally set up when the system is installed. It usually cannot be added afterwards without installing again, which erases the disk.

> Note: This is an early test version, and we have not yet confirmed and documented an encryption option in the installer. If this matters to you, please ask through [Report a problem](https://oneclickos.org/report) before you install. See [Install OneClick OS on a PC](/getting-started/install-on-a-pc).

> Warning: If you do use disk encryption and forget the passphrase, the files cannot be recovered by anyone. Keep the passphrase somewhere safe, and keep a backup.

## What you can do today

- **Use a strong account password** and do not set the PC to sign in automatically. See [Change your password](/settings/password).
- **Lock the screen when you leave the PC.** Hold the Windows key and press **L**.
- **Keep the most private files off the laptop.** Store them in a cloud drive that is protected by its own sign-in, or on a drive you keep at home.
- **Protect your online accounts.** Signing out of your email and cloud accounts remotely, and changing those passwords, limits the damage if the PC is taken.
- **Keep a backup.** Losing the PC should not mean losing the files. See [Back up your files](/updates/back-up-your-files).

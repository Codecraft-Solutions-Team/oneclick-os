A notification is a small message that slides in to tell you something, such as a finished download or a drive being plugged in.

## Where they appear

- In **Windows style**, notifications slide in at the bottom right of the screen, above the clock.
- In **Mac style**, they appear at the top right.

A notification stays for about five seconds and then goes away by itself. Some have buttons, such as one to open a file that has finished downloading. Click the notification or its button to act on it, or close it with its close button.

## See ones you missed

Notifications you did not act on are kept for a while. Click the notifications icon among the small icons near the clock to see them. If you cannot see that icon, click the small arrow beside the icons to show the hidden ones.

From that panel you can clear the list.

## Stop being interrupted

The same panel has a do not disturb switch. While it is on, notifications do not pop up. They are still collected in the list for you to read later. Remember to turn it off again.

## Choose which apps can notify you

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Search for `notifications` and open that page.
3. Change how notifications behave, or turn them off for a single app.

## What OneClick itself sends

OneClick OS does not use notifications for advertising or tips. You will see them for practical things, such as the progress of the starter apps being installed after the welcome tour.

OneClick does not download or install a system update in the background, so a notification will never tell you that it restarted for one. See [How updates work](/updates/how-updates-work).

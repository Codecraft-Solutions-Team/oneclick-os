OneClick OS uses a small amount of movement to show what is happening. You can speed it up or turn it off completely.

## What moves

- Windows grow slightly as they open and shrink as they close.
- A minimised window shrinks towards the taskbar. In Mac style it is drawn down into the dock.
- Notifications and small panels slide in from the edge of the screen.
- The pointer and the taskbar icon show that an app is starting.
- The Start menu rises into place, and buttons react when the pointer is over them.

Effects that many people find distracting, such as windows that wobble when dragged, are turned off from the start.

## Turn animations off

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Search for `animation speed`.
3. Drag the **Animation speed** slider all the way to **Instant**.
4. Apply the change.

With the slider at **Instant**, windows, menus and notifications appear and disappear straight away. This one setting covers the whole desktop, including the Start menu and OneClick's own Settings pages.

To keep some movement but make it quicker, stop the slider part of the way along.

## Why you might want this

- Motion on screen makes some people feel unwell or distracted.
- On an older or slower PC, turning animations off can make the desktop feel quicker.
- If you use OneClick in a virtual machine or over a remote connection, animations can look jerky.

> Note: Some apps draw their own animations and do not follow this setting. Games and web pages are the usual examples.

## If the PC still feels slow

Animations are rarely the main cause. See [The PC feels slow](/troubleshooting/slow).

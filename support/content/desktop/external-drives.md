USB sticks, external hard drives and memory cards work without installing anything.

## Open a drive

1. Plug the drive in.
2. A small message appears near the corner of the taskbar offering to open it. Click it.
3. If you miss the message, open **Files**. The drive is listed on the left side under its own name. Click it.

Drives do not get a letter such as D or E. They are shown by name. See [Where is the C drive](/windows/drive-letters).

## Copy files to or from it

Open the drive in **Files**, then drag files in or out, or use **Ctrl+C** and **Ctrl+V**. See [Find and manage your files](/desktop/files).

## Remove it safely

Do not pull a drive out while files are still being copied. Tell the PC you are finished first:

1. In **Files**, find the drive on the left side.
2. Click the eject button beside its name, or right-click it and choose the option to safely remove it.
3. Wait until the drive disappears from the list or a message says it is safe, then unplug it.

> Warning: Unplugging a drive while it is still writing can damage the files on it. Copying can carry on for a few seconds after the progress bar has gone, so always eject first.

## Drives you used with Windows

Drives formatted for Windows, including the common FAT32, exFAT and NTFS types, can normally be opened here. You do not need to reformat a drive to use it on both systems.

If a drive was not ejected properly in Windows, or Windows was hibernating with it attached, it may open as read-only or not at all. Plug it back into Windows, eject it properly, and try again.

To reach the Windows drive inside the same PC, see [Open your Windows drive from OneClick](/windows/read-windows-drives).

Mac style rearranges the desktop to work like a Mac. To turn it on or off, see [Switch between Windows style and Mac style](/desktop/look-windows-or-mac).

> Note: Mac style is new in this test version. Some parts may not be finished.

## The menu bar at the top

A thin bar runs along the top of the screen.

- At the left end is the same Start menu button as in Windows style. Click it to search, see all your apps, or shut down. See [The Start menu](/desktop/start-menu).
- Next to it are the menus of the app you are using, such as File and Edit. They change when you switch apps. Some apps keep their menus inside their own window instead.
- At the right end are the small icons for Wi-Fi, sound and so on, and the clock.

## The dock at the bottom

The dock is a rounded bar that floats in the middle of the bottom edge. It holds your pinned apps and any other apps that are open.

- Click an icon to open the app or switch to it.
- Icons grow as the pointer passes over them.
- Right-click an icon for a short menu: **Open** or **Open new window**, **Pin to dock** or **Unpin from dock**, **Minimize** or **Restore**, and **Close**.

To begin with the dock holds your web browser, **Files**, **App Store**, **Settings** and **Update**.

To change the size of the dock or make it hide, open **Settings** and choose **Taskbar**. See [Change how the taskbar behaves](/desktop/taskbar-settings).

## Window buttons

The three window buttons are round and sit at the top left of each window. From left to right they are close, minimise and maximise.

When you minimise a window, it is drawn down into the dock. Click its dock icon to bring it back.

## Other differences

- Notifications appear at the top right.
- Bars and menus are slightly see-through, with the background blurred behind them.
- The bottom-right corner still shows the desktop if you rest the pointer there for about a second.

Everything else is the same in both styles: your files, your apps, Settings and updates.

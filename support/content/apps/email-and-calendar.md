OneClick OS has no account of its own and does not set up email for you. You choose between two ways of reading mail.

## In the browser

This needs no setup. Open your web browser and go to the site you already use, such as Gmail, Outlook.com, Yahoo Mail or your internet provider's webmail. Sign in as you did on Windows. Your mail, contacts and calendar are all there, because they are kept by the email service and not on the PC.

To get to it quickly, bookmark the page in your browser.

## In an app

If you prefer a mail program like Outlook or Windows Mail, install **Thunderbird**. It is free and handles email, calendars and contacts for one account or several.

1. Open the **App Store** from the Start menu.
2. Search for **Thunderbird** and press **Install**.
3. Open Thunderbird from the Start menu.
4. Enter your name, email address and password when it asks. For the big email services it finds the right settings by itself.

Thunderbird is also offered in the welcome tour, in the Work starter set. See [Choose your starter apps](/getting-started/choose-starter-apps).

> Note: Gmail and Microsoft accounts open a sign-in page from Google or Microsoft inside Thunderbird. That is expected. Work and school accounts are sometimes locked down by the organisation. If yours will not connect, use the browser.

## Outlook

The Outlook desktop program is part of Microsoft Office and does not run on OneClick OS. Outlook on the web does, and so does adding your Outlook.com address to Thunderbird. See [Microsoft Office](/windows/microsoft-office).

## Moving old mail

If your mail is with Gmail, Outlook.com or a similar service, there is nothing to move. Sign in and it is all there. Mail that was stored only on your old PC, for example in an Outlook data file, has to be exported on Windows first. See [Move your files from Windows](/windows/move-your-files).

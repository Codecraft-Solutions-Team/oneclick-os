On Windows you search the web for an installer and hope the site is the real one. OneClick OS works more like a phone. Apps come from one catalogue, through the **App Store**.

## One public app catalogue

The App Store installs apps from a large public catalogue made for systems like this one. Each app arrives in its own separate bundle so it stays apart from the rest of the system.

OneClick OS does not run this catalogue and does not review the apps in it. The App Store is a window onto that catalogue. (In web searches you may see the catalogue called Flathub at [flathub.org](https://flathub.org) and its app format called Flatpak, though you do not need either name to use the App Store.)

## Why this is safer than downloading installers

- **One source.** You are not downloading from a look-alike website or an advert at the top of a search.
- **Apps are kept apart.** Each app is installed in its own space and cannot change the system itself.
- **The system is separate.** Installing or removing an app does not touch the system. System updates are handled on their own, see [How updates work](/updates/how-updates-work).
- **No bundled extras.** Installing one app does not add toolbars or other programs.

## What to still be careful about

No catalogue is perfect. A few sensible habits help:

- Prefer well-known apps. Many apps in the catalogue are marked as verified, which means the people who make the app publish it there themselves.
- Read the app's page before you install it.
- Be wary of any website that tells you to paste a command into the Terminal.

> Note: Apps in the App Store are made by many different people and companies. Some big-name apps are packaged by volunteers, not by the company that makes them. The app's page says who publishes it.

For more on staying safe, see [Viruses and staying safe](/updates/viruses-and-security). To see why installers from websites are not used here, see [Why you do not download installers from websites](/windows/installing-is-different).

## Give it a few minutes after starting

Just after you sign in, the PC can be busy. On the first sign-in it installs the apps you chose in the welcome tour. After that, things settle.

## Restart

If the PC has been on for days, restart it. Use **Restart** in the **Start menu**.

## See what is using the PC

1. Open **Task Manager** from the **Start menu**.
2. Look at which apps are using the most processor and memory.
3. Close the ones you do not need. If one is stuck at the top and will not close, select it and end it.

See [Task Manager](/desktop/task-manager).

## Common causes

- **Too many browser tabs.** Each one uses memory. Close the ones you are finished with.
- **Not enough memory.** With 4 GB, a browser and one or two other apps is about the limit. With 8 GB or more this is rarely the cause.
- **A nearly full disk.** See [The disk is full](/troubleshooting/out-of-space).
- **A download or update in progress.** An update downloading in the background uses the disk and the internet for a while.
- **An old hard disk.** A PC with a spinning hard disk is much slower to start and open apps than one with a solid-state disk. That is the hardware, not OneClick OS.

## Turn the animations down

Windows opening and closing are animated. On an older PC, turning this down can make things feel quicker. See [Turn animations down or off](/desktop/animations).

## Slow in games or video

That usually points at the graphics card. See [Get better performance in games](/gaming/game-performance). On a PC with Nvidia graphics, check you have the Nvidia version. See [Which version for Nvidia graphics](/getting-started/nvidia-version).

## Slow everywhere, all the time

If even moving a window is jerky, the graphics card may not be supported yet and the PC is drawing the screen the slow way.

> Note: OneClick OS is an early test version and hardware support varies. Please [report the problem](/troubleshooting/report-a-problem) with the make and model of the PC.
